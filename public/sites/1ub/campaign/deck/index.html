Our understanding of the business

1U intends to serve both sides of the utility economy.

1Utility Bank is introducing Utility Banking, a banking proposition built around the essential services people and businesses depend on, and the organisations and infrastructure that provide them. At the consumer level, its defining product is the Utility Account: an account linked to a customer’s phone number that allows money sent by bank transfer to become airtime, data or other supported utility payments. Around this proposition, 1U intends to provide a broader suite of personal and business banking services, including savings, investments and intelligent security controls. On the institutional side, the bank also intends to finance utility providers, assets, infrastructure and renewable-energy projects.

Our understanding of the challenge

1U must introduce a new category and earn permission to own it.

The campaign must do more than launch another digital financial product. It must help Nigerians understand what Utility Banking is, why it matters and why they should trust 1U to deliver it. That breaks down into four connected communication tasks.

Explain

Make Utility Banking and the Utility Account immediately understandable.

Differentiate

Establish 1U as neither another fintech nor another traditional bank.

Reassure

Show the service is secure, dependable and supported by a complete banking ecosystem.

Convert

Move people from awareness to a first utility payment, then into repeated use.

Make a new financial category feel familiar, useful and trustworthy from day one.

Our understanding of the audience

Different Nigerians, connected by the same everyday reality.

The brief identifies a broad audience: salaried professionals, students, SMEs, traders, gig workers, families, young professionals and digitally confident consumers. Incomes and lifestyles differ. What they share is that money is constantly being assigned to practical responsibilities such as electricity, airtime, data, transport, rent, healthcare, school fees, family support and business needs.

Questions for alignment

What we need to know before we finalise the plan.

  1. What is 1U's precise regulatory status, and what licensing language should appear in campaign communications?
  2. Have "Africa's first Utility Bank," "automatically part of our network" and "patented innovation" been legally approved as public claims?
  3. Which Utility Account functions will be live at launch: airtime, data, electricity, water, television, transport, rent, school fees or others?
  4. Does every phone number already have a usable Utility Account, or must the owner register and complete KYC before receiving value?
  5. What happens when money is sent to a phone number belonging to someone who has not registered with 1U?
  6. What is the priority conversion event: account registration, completed KYC, first deposit or first utility payment?
  7. Which audience should lead the launch? The current target definition covers several groups with different needs and motivations.
  8. Is the campaign launching nationally or in selected Nigerian states and cities first?
  1. Which product should lead communications after Utility Banking: personal banking, business banking, savings, investments or utility financing?
  2. What evidence can substantiate trust at launch: transaction success rates, security certifications, reversal policies, service guarantees or banking partnerships?
  3. What customer support and dispute resolution systems will be available when a utility payment fails or is delayed?
  4. Are there approved launch offers, cashback, referral rewards or transaction incentives?
  5. What media and production budget should guide the proposed campaign scale?
  6. What are the confirmed launch date, campaign duration and product readiness milestones?
  7. Which brand assets, tone of voice rules and mandatory legal disclaimers will agencies receive?
